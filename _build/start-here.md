## I want to get better at following an argument

Start with **Thinking Like a Philosopher** for reasoning, assumptions and thought experiments. If your question is how we justify what we believe, **What Can We Know?** takes you further into knowledge and doubt.

[Explore Thinking Like a Philosopher](/books/#thinking-like-a-philosopher) · [Explore What Can We Know?](/books/#what-can-we-know)

## I keep wondering whether our choices are free

**Free Will and Determinism** introduces the debate about choice and responsibility. It gives you a way into the disagreement over what would make an action genuinely yours.

[Explore Free Will and Determinism](/books/#free-will-and-determinism)

## I want to understand competing ideas of fairness

**What Is Justice?** offers the wider philosophical question. **John Rawls** concentrates on one influential response, including the original position and veil of ignorance.

[Explore What Is Justice?](/books/#what-is-justice) · [Explore John Rawls](/books/#john-rawls)

## I wonder why we argue about art

**What Makes Something Beautiful?** follows questions about taste and aesthetic judgment. **Aesthetics** offers a broader route into the philosophy of art, beauty and the sublime.

[Explore What Makes Something Beautiful?](/books/#what-makes-something-beautiful) · [Explore Aesthetics](/books/#aesthetics)

## I’d like to spend time with one thinker

**Albert Camus** explores the absurd, rebellion and human dignity. **Simone Weil** follows questions about attention, suffering and our obligations to others. Choose the questions you want to sit with first.

[Explore Albert Camus](/books/#albert-camus) · [Explore Simone Weil](/books/#simone-weil)
